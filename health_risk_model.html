📋 Project Overview

As part of my CENG401 Capstone Project at Ankara Yıldırım Beyazıt University, I led a three-person team to develop an AI-powered early warning and decision support system for chronic disease prediction and hospital resource optimization.

💡 The Problem

Noncommunicable diseases (diabetes, hypertension, stroke) are leading causes of death worldwide. Most prediction systems rely on expensive laboratory tests, limiting accessibility. Patients often seek help too late, while others consume resources unnecessarily — creating hospital overload and staff burnout.

🎯 Our Solution

A two-stage machine learning pipeline that predicts individual disease risk using only easily accessible, self-reported health indicators. The system prioritizes clinical validity and patient safety over flashy accuracy metrics.

🏆 Key Achievements

🔬 Three Disease Models

Diabetes: 85% accuracy on 250K+ BRFSS dataset (XGBoost + calibration)
Hypertension: 82% Logistic Regression chosen over 98% overfitted XGBoost
Stroke: ANN with optimized 0.125 threshold for early detection

🧬 Synthetic Data Innovation

Generated 30,000 medically-plausible synthetic patient records combining physician expertise with AI-assisted validation.
Risk formula: Effect = (Impact / 100) × Effectiveness

🏥 Clinical-First Design

Three-tier "traffic light" risk stratification (Green/Yellow/Red). Percentile-based scoring (0-100) for intuitive communication. Probability calibration ensuring predicted risks match actual frequencies.

⚙️ Two-Stage Architecture

Stage 1: Rapid screening with 12 most important features
Stage 2: Detailed assessment with disease-specific models
Enables both mass screening and personalized evaluation

🔬 Clinical Validity Over Accuracy

Our most important technical decision: choosing the right model, not the highest-scoring one.

✅ Logistic Regression (82%)

  • Healthy person → 10.8% risk (correct)
  • Clinically interpretable coefficients
  • Generalizes well to unseen data
  • Consistent across patient profiles
  • Fits clinical decision-making needs

❌ XGBoost (98%)

  • Healthy person → 79.2% risk (dangerous!)
  • Severely overfitted to training data
  • Black-box predictions
  • 98% train accuracy masks poor generalization
  • Could cause unnecessary panic/treatment

⚠️ Critical Insight

In medical applications, a model that generalizes well and makes clinical sense is infinitely more valuable than one with high test accuracy. The XGBoost model was predicting a 79.2% hypertension risk for completely healthy patients — a potentially dangerous clinical outcome.

Technical Implementation

  • Handled severe class imbalance: 84% healthy in diabetes, 95% non-stroke
  • Applied probability calibration using CalibratedClassifierCV
  • Used precision-recall curves for clinically appropriate threshold optimization
  • ANN stroke model: 64→32→16 Dense layers with Batch Normalization
  • Stroke decision threshold: 0.125 (not standard 0.5) — prioritizes sensitivity

🧬 Synthetic Data Pipeline

To address data limitations, we built a human-in-the-loop synthetic data generation pipeline combining physician expertise with AI-assisted validation.

📊 30,000 Patient Records

Each record includes demographics, lifestyle factors, and health indicators — all medically plausible and validated by domain experts.

🔬 Effect-Based Risk Formula

Effect = (Impact / 100) × Effectiveness
Combines physician-assessed impact values with statistical effectiveness measures for realistic risk distributions.

👨‍⚕️ Expert Integration

🔧 Model Details

💉 Diabetes (XGBoost)

Dataset: 250K+ BRFSS records
Accuracy: 85% · ROC-AUC: 0.827
Imbalance: 84% healthy cases handled via scale_pos_weight
Calibration: CalibratedClassifierCV applied

❤️ Hypertension (Logistic Regression)

Decision: Chose 82% LR over 98% XGBoost
Why: XGBoost predicted 79.2% risk for healthy patients
Benefit: Interpretable coefficients for clinical use
Validation: Consistent across diverse patient profiles

🧠 Stroke (Artificial Neural Network)

Architecture: 64→32→16 Dense + BatchNorm
Threshold: 0.125 (not 0.5) — prioritizes early detection
Imbalance: 95% non-stroke cases
Accuracy: 93-94% with optimized sensitivity

🎓 Key Lessons Learned

📐 Generalization > Overfitting

In healthcare, a model that performs consistently across populations is worth far more than one with inflated test metrics.

🏥 Clinical Soundness > High Accuracy

An 82% model that gives safe predictions beats a 98% model that endangers patients through overconfident, incorrect risk estimates.

🤝 Human + AI = Better Results

Physician-validated synthetic data and clinician-guided threshold decisions produced more reliable models than pure ML optimization.

📉 Threshold Optimization Matters

Moving the stroke threshold from 0.5 to 0.125 dramatically improved early detection — most practitioners overlook this critical step.
